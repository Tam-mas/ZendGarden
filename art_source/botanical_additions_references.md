# Botanical additions — species and visual references

Thirty append-only catalogue entries, IDs 106–135. References inform leaf shape, arrangement, ribs, pads, areoles, spines and flowering habit. All artwork is authored geometry and generated pigment maps; reference photographs are not redistributed. Heights represent garden/container specimens, not the largest wild plants. Game growing days and watering are the existing simplified gardening system, not horticultural prescriptions.

## Revised climbers

Sweet pea (ID 9, *Lathyrus odoratus*) retains paired leaflets and tendrils, with explicitly shaped banner, wing and keel petals. Clematis (ID 10, a large-flowered garden form) retains compound leaves and now has six outward-facing petaloid sepals around a tuft of cream stamens. Triangulated curved petals avoid pinched or twisted quads, and retain opaque, double-sided materials. Existing planting IDs and root envelopes are preserved. References: [Sweet pea](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/lathyrus-odoratus/), [Clematis](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/clematis/).

## Source and rebuild

`botanical_additions_data.py` records the names and representative forms. `botanical_additions_forms.py` builds the organs, and `build_botanical_additions.py` exports the 30 additions and revised climbers into `botanical_additions.blend`. `build_plant_growth.py` reads that library after the earlier libraries, so revised IDs replace their earlier source forms. Run Blender in a separate approved OS process. Both builders accept `-- --ids` to limit exports during iteration. Run the additions builder after the original and expansion builders.

Cactus and succulent materials do not receive leaf wind, and their saved per-instance bends and twists are restrained. Their seedlings use the same species-specific ribs, pads or fleshy-leaf forms at smaller sizes. The 300-pixel ornate sidebar has a two-line category button; the collection can search both common and botanical names.
