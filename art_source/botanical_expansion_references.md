# Botanical expansion: reference and modelling notes

46 appended entries (IDs 60–105), researched 27 September 2026. The original
60 IDs and groundcover category are retained. These are original procedural
Blender meshes and painted/procedural PBR maps, not downloaded plant assets.
The models depict representative garden specimens at game scale. Giant trees
are reduced in absolute height; they retain distinct trunk/crown proportions.
Colour, seasons and growth times are representative game choices, not a
horticultural simulation of cultivar, climate or maturity variation.

## Flowers (60–67)

- Common jasmine (*Jasminum officinale*): compound leaves, slender scrambling
  stems, small white five-pointed flowers.
- Pink jasmine (*J. polyanthum*): white open flowers and long pink buds; the
  pink does not recolour the entire open flower.
- Winter jasmine (*J. nudiflorum*): arching green stems, sparse trifoliate
  foliage, six-lobed yellow flowers. Only common/pink jasmine use climbing
  support gameplay.
- Dahlia ‘Café au Lait’: concentric, densely layered cream/blush petals.
- Tulip ‘Queen of Night’: dark purple-black cupped flower, six overlapping
  tepals and broad strap leaves.
- Blue delphinium: lobed foliage, blue racemes, pale flower centres and spurs.
- Purple lupin: palmate leaflets and whorled pea flowers on tapered spikes.
- Burgundy hollyhock: tall stems, round lobed foliage, large open flowers and
  projecting staminal columns.

References: [RHS jasmine guide](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/jasmine/growing-guide),
[RHS pink jasmine](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/9457/jasminum-polyanthum/details),
[RHS seed descriptions including Lupinus](https://apps.rhs.org.uk/ecommerce/PDF/seedlist2017.pdf).

## Bamboos (68–72 and 80)

All have hollow-looking segmented culms, raised nodes, sheath scars, branching
at nodes and narrow lance-shaped foliage. Game categories reflect size, so
these do not add entries to the groundcover/Grasses category.

- Slender weaver’s bamboo (*Bambusa textilis* var. *gracilis*): narrow upright
  green clump.
- Golden bamboo (*Phyllostachys aurea*): golden mature culms, green foliage.
- Black bamboo (*P. nigra*): dark mature culms interspersed with green young ones.
- Himalayan blue bamboo (*Himalayacalamus hookerianus*): blue-grey waxy culms.
- Red bamboo ‘Jiuzhaigou’ (*Fargesia*): finer red culms and airy foliage. Red
  pigmentation is a representative seasonal appearance, not a seasonal shader.
- Giant bamboo (*Dendrocalamus giganteus*): taller clump, much thicker culms
  and more prominent node collars.

References: [Bambusa gracilis](https://www.bambooland.com.au/bambusa-textilis-var-gracilis),
[RHS blue bamboo](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/103090/himalayacalamus-hookerianus-blue-bamboo/details),
[RHS Jiuzhaigou](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/524636/fargesia-jiuzhaigou-red-dragon/details),
[RHS bamboo overview](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/types/grasses/bamboo-control),
[Kew giant bamboo](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn%3Alsid%3Aipni.org%3Anames%3A397880-1/general-information).

## Roses (73–77)

Pinnate toothed leaflets, branching canes, thorns, sepals and buds are modelled
separately from petals. Flower geometry differs among cultivars:

- ‘Mister Lincoln’: high-centred deep crimson flower.
- ‘Iceberg’: clustered smaller white flowers with more exposed anthers.
- ‘Golden Celebration’: tightly layered golden cupped rosettes.
- ‘Just Joey’: shorter shrub, broad ruffled apricot petals.
- ‘Scentimental’: irregular cream stripes across red petals, baked into the
  colour map rather than alternating uniformly coloured petals.

References: [RHS Mister Lincoln](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/94618/rosa-mister-lincoln-ht/details),
[RHS Just Joey](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/119725/rosa-just-joey-(ht)/details),
[David Austin Golden Celebration](https://www.davidaustinroses.com/products/golden-celebration),
[Weeks Roses Scentimental](https://www.weeksroses.com/varieties/p/scentimental).

## Trees (78–91)

- Giant redwood (*Sequoiadendron giganteum*): broad buttressed reddish trunk,
  conical crown and short awl/scale foliage in three-dimensional sprays.
- Coast redwood (*Sequoia sempervirens*): taller, narrower trunk and flattened
  two-ranked needle sprays. Both have textured cones and fibrous bark.
- Ginkgo: notched fans with radial ribs; a green-leaved specimen.
- Fig: deeply lobed broad leaves and necked purple figs.
- Pear: green-gold fruit with a narrower neck, broad toothed leaves.
- Peach and nectarine: long narrow serrated leaves, red-yellow fruit and a
  longitudinal crease. Shared stone-fruit structure with different pigments.
- Plum: purple fruit and toothed oval leaves.
- Apricot: rounder broad leaves and orange fruit with a crease.
- Orange, mandarin and lime: glossy evergreen leaves; orange, flattened
  mandarin and green lime fruit respectively.
- Avocado: larger entire leaves and dark, elongated pear-shaped fruit.

Ripe-fruit trees are depicted at fruiting stage, without an implausible full
flush of spring blossom at the same time. No new seasonal leaf-drop system is
introduced by these assets.

References: [US National Park Service giant sequoias](https://www.nps.gov/seki/learn/nature/bigtrees.htm),
[US Forest Service redwood comparison](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/r05/sequoia/publication/All%20about%20the%20Redwoods%202024.pdf),
[RHS Ginkgo](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/7990/ginkgo-biloba/details).

## Australian natives (92–96)

- Snow gum (*Eucalyptus pauciflora* subsp. *pauciflora*): branching smooth trunks,
  cream/grey/olive patches and hanging lance-shaped leaves.
- Alpine snow gum (*E. pauciflora* subsp. *niphophila*): lower multi-stem habit.
- Red flowering gum (*Corymbia ficifolia*): rounded tree with individual red
  stamens forming terminal clusters, plus woody capsules.
- Waratah (*Telopea speciosissima*): red terminal heads assembled from many
  small tubular florets, surrounding bracts and toothed leathery leaves.
- Gymea lily (*Doryanthes excelsa*): basal sword-leaf rosette, tall flowering
  stalk and compact terminal cluster of red trumpet-shaped flowers.

References: [ANBG cold-climate eucalypts](https://www.anbg.gov.au/gnp/eucalypts-cold-climates.html),
[ANBG snow gum](https://test.anbg.gov.au/gnp/interns-2003/eucalyptus-pauciflora.html),
[ANBG waratah](https://anbg.gov.au/gnp/interns-2011/telopea-speciosissima.html),
[ANBG Gymea lily](https://anbg.gov.au/gnp/gnp12/doryanthes-excelsa.html).

## Produce (97–105)

- Globe artichoke: deeply cut silver-green basal leaves and layered pointed
  bud bracts.
- Rhubarb: thick red petioles and broad puckered leaves.
- Asparagus: scaly pointed spears beside finely divided mature fern foliage.
- Broccoli: green central head built from small bud clusters (calabrese form).
- Cauliflower: ivory curd, nested florets and surrounding upright leaves.
- Capsicum: lobed hanging red and green fruit, simple leaves and small white
  flowers.
- Brussels sprouts: small leafy buds along the upright stalk, at leaf axils,
  with a leafy top.
- Leek: pale cylindrical shaft with flat, blue-green leaves in opposing fans.
- Hemp (*Cannabis sativa*): tall branching stem, seven narrow distinctly
  serrated palmate leaflets and inconspicuous green flower clusters.

References: [RHS broccoli/calabrese distinction](https://www.rhs.org.uk/vegetables/broccoli/grow-your-own),
[RHS Brussels sprouts](https://www.rhs.org.uk/vegetables/brussels-sprouts/grow-your-own),
[RHS hemp](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/30522/cannabis-sativa/details).

## Reproducibility and scope

`build_botanical_expansion.py` produces the 46 GLBs and
`botanical_expansion.blend`. Each plant has deterministic seeded variation;
Foliage and Bloom groups preserve growth, harvesting and wind integration.
Normal, roughness and pigment maps are embedded in each GLB and packed into
the source library. Model portraits are rendered by Godot, so the review sheets
show the game's actual imported meshes/materials rather than concept art.

The original library builder still builds IDs 0–59. `rebuild.sh` invokes both
builders in order. New IDs append to the original catalogue and do not alter
old save references. Native GPU and Compatibility rendering should both be
reviewed before shipping. Dense gardens on physical mobile hardware require
separate performance profiling.
